# Six Phases of Creation: science dossier

Research notes for a film in six phases, from the Big Bang to the Earth before humans. Every date below is the current mainstream scientific figure, with its source. Where scientists still disagree, the note says so. Use this as the fact base for narration and on-screen text.

Snapshot: October 2026. Re-check "record" claims (most distant galaxy, first-star detections) before publishing, because they change often.

## The phases at a glance

| # | Phase | When (years ago) | When (after the Big Bang) | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Big Bang and the first matter | 13.8 billion | 0 to 380,000 years | Space expands from a hot, dense state. The first protons, neutrons and nuclei (hydrogen, helium) form, then the first atoms. Light breaks free. |
| 2 | Birth and death of the first stars | ~13.6 to 13.0 billion | ~100 million to ~1 billion years | The "dark ages" end. Giant first stars ignite, live a few million years, and explode, making the first carbon, oxygen and iron. |
| 3 | Galactic disks form | ~13 to 8 billion | ~1 to 6 billion years | Gas settles into rotating disks inside dark-matter halos. The Milky Way's disk takes shape. |
| 4 | The solar system forms | 4.567 billion | ~9.2 billion years | A gas cloud collapses into the Sun and a planet-forming disk. Earth forms, and a collision makes the Moon. |
| 5 | The first layers of the atmosphere | ~4.5 to ~0.5 billion | | A steam and CO2 atmosphere, oceans, a methane haze, then oxygen and the ozone layer, which creates the layered sky we have today. |
| 6 | Earth's transformations before humans | ~4.5 billion to ~300,000 | | Supercontinents assemble and break, the planet freezes over and thaws, life explodes, five mass extinctions. |

Scale aid for the edit: if the universe's 13.8 billion years were one calendar year, the Sun forms on **1 September**, oxygen fills the air around **28 October**, dinosaurs die on **30 December**, and *Homo sapiens* appears in the **last 12 minutes of 31 December**. (Each day of this calendar is ~37.8 million years.)

## Phase 1: The Big Bang and the first matter

### Before the Big Bang (be honest on screen)
- Physics does not currently describe "before." Known laws break down at the **Planck time (~10⁻⁴³ s)**. Narration should say "we don't know" rather than show a fake "before" image. Candidate ideas (eternal inflation, bouncing universes, "no-boundary" proposals) are hypotheses with no observational support yet.
- Good line: *the Big Bang was not an explosion in space; it was the expansion of space itself, everywhere at once.*

### The sequence
| Time after the Big Bang | Event |
|---|---|
| ~10⁻³⁶ to 10⁻³² s | **Inflation**: space expands exponentially. Tiny quantum ripples are stretched into the seeds of all later structure. |
| ~10⁻⁶ s (1 microsecond) | Quarks bind into **protons and neutrons**. |
| ~1 s to ~20 min | **Big Bang nucleosynthesis**: about **75% hydrogen and 25% helium** by mass, plus traces of lithium. After about five minutes most of today's helium exists, and the universe has cooled too much to make more. ([ESA](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/So_how_did_everything_start), [NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/Universe/overview)) |
| ~380,000 years | **Recombination**: the universe cools to ~3,000 K, electrons join nuclei, and light travels freely for the first time. That light is the **cosmic microwave background (CMB)**, now cooled to 2.725 K. |

### Ingredients of the universe (Planck 2018)
- **~5% ordinary matter** (everything made of atoms), **~27% dark matter**, **~68% dark energy**.
- Age: **13.787 ± 0.020 billion years** ([Planck 2018 results I](https://www.iac.es/en/science-and-technology/publications/planck-2018-results-i-overview-and-cosmological-legacy-planck)).
- Note for accuracy: the "Hubble tension" (the local expansion rate disagrees with the CMB-derived rate) is unresolved. It does not change the 13.8-billion-year headline.

### The evidence (what we can actually show)
- The CMB maps from **COBE (1989) → WMAP (2001) → Planck (2009)**, each sharper than the last ([NASA/JPL PIA16874](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia16874-the-universe-comes-into-sharper-focus/)). The hot and cold spots are the inflation ripples, frozen in place.
- The measured hydrogen and helium ratio matches the nucleosynthesis prediction.

## Phase 2: Birth and death of the first stars

### The cosmic dark ages (~380,000 to ~100-200 million years)
After recombination the universe is transparent but there are no stars yet. It is dark and filled with neutral hydrogen. Gravity pulls gas into dark-matter clumps.

### Population III, the first stars
- Made only of hydrogen and helium (no heavier "metals"). Theory says they were **massive, roughly 10-100+ solar masses**, very hot and blue, and **lived only a few million years**.
- They ignited **~100-250 million years** after the Big Bang. NASA's illustration of this era shows first-star clusters at **~200-400 million years**.
- **2026 evidence:** JWST found helium and hydrogen emission with no heavier elements near galaxy **GN-z11** (~400 million years after the Big Bang). That is the strongest case yet for Population III stars, implying masses of 10-100 Suns ([phys.org, Apr 2026](https://phys.org/news/2026-04-astronomers-strongest-evidence-universe-stars.html); [AAS Nova](https://aasnova.org/2026/04/21/a-possible-glimpse-of-the-universes-first-stars/)). Call it "strongest evidence", not "confirmed".
- **Most distant galaxy (as of Oct 2026):** **MoM-z14**, redshift 14.44, seen **~280 million years** after the Big Bang (JWST, 2025) ([Sky at Night](https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/news/webb-galaxy-mom-z14)).

### Their deaths, and why they matter to us
- First stars died as **supernovae** (the most massive possibly as "pair-instability" supernovae that leave nothing behind) or collapsed into **black holes**.
- The explosions made and scattered the **first carbon, oxygen, silicon and iron**: every heavy atom in Earth and in your body came from generations of stars that lived and died before the Sun. Present-day analog: **Cassiopeia A**, where Webb shows freshly made oxygen, argon and neon ([NASA Webb](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/webb/webb-reveals-never-before-seen-details-in-cassiopeia-a/)).
- Their ultraviolet light **reionized** the universe's hydrogen. Reionization was complete by **~1 billion years**.

## Phase 3: Galactic disks form

### How a disk forms
Gas falls into a dark-matter halo, collides, radiates away energy and cools. It keeps its spin (angular momentum), so it flattens into a rotating **disk**, the same physics that flattens pizza dough when it spins. Stars form in the disk, spiral arms appear, and bars can form.

### Recent discoveries (the story got earlier)
- **The Milky Way's thin disk began less than 1 billion years after the Big Bang.** Gaia found many thin-disk stars older than 10 billion years, some older than 13 billion. Earlier estimates said 8-9 billion years ago ([AIP / Gaia-RVS study, arXiv:2402.00561](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.00561); [Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/astronomy/milky-ways-thin-disk-13142.html)).
- The old disk existed before the Milky Way's last major merger with the **Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus** dwarf galaxy, ~9-10 billion years ago.
- **ceers-2112**: JWST found a barred spiral like the Milky Way only **~2 billion years** after the Big Bang (Nature, 2023). Astronomers had expected galaxies that young to be too chaotic for this ([UC Riverside](https://cnas.ucr.edu/media/2023/11/14/webb-telescope-spots-most-distant-milky-way-galaxy-yet)).

### Milky Way facts for on-screen text
- ~100,000 light-years across, **100-400 billion stars**. The Sun sits ~26,000 light-years from the centre and orbits it about once every ~230 million years.

## Phase 4: The solar system forms

| When | Event |
|---|---|
| **4.567 billion years ago** | The oldest solid grains (calcium-aluminium-rich inclusions, or "CAIs", in meteorites like **Allende**) form. Their uranium-lead age, **4,567.3 million years**, is the official age of the solar system. Most CAIs formed within **~40,000-200,000 years** ([LLNL](https://www.llnl.gov/news/solar-system-formed-less-200000-years)). |
| Trigger | A nearby **supernova** shock wave probably compressed part of a molecular cloud and started the collapse. |
| First few million years | The Sun ignites. A **protoplanetary disk** of gas and dust forms rings and gaps where planets grow. Jupiter forms early. Real analog: ALMA's **HL Tauri** image, a star under 1 million years old with gaps at the scale of Neptune's and Pluto's orbits ([ESO eso1436](https://eso.org/public/news/eso1436/)). |
| ~10-100 million years | Rocky planets assemble from collisions. Earth reaches its final size; its accepted age is **~4.54 billion years**. |
| ~4.5 billion years ago | **The Moon-forming impact.** A Mars-sized body (**Theia**) hits the young Earth. A 2022 NASA Ames/Durham supercomputer simulation suggests the Moon could have formed **within hours** ([EarthSky](https://earthsky.org/space/collision-may-have-formed-the-moon-mere-hours/)). |

## Phase 5: The first layers of the atmosphere

Key idea for the script: **the layered sky we see from space is a product of life.** The stratosphere exists because ozone absorbs ultraviolet light and heats that layer. Ozone needs oxygen, and the oxygen came from microbes.

| When | Atmosphere stage |
|---|---|
| ~4.5 billion years ago | **Primary atmosphere** of hydrogen and helium captured from the disk, mostly lost to the young Sun's wind and to impacts. |
| ~4.5-4.4 billion years ago | **Secondary atmosphere** from volcanic outgassing (and comet and asteroid delivery): **steam, CO₂, nitrogen**, little or no free oxygen. |
| **~4.4 billion years ago** | **Liquid water and oceans.** Jack Hills zircons (Western Australia), the oldest Earth material, carry oxygen isotopes showing contact with liquid water ([Eos](https://eos.org/articles/four-billion-year-old-zircons-may-contain-our-earliest-evidence-of-fresh-water); [UW-Madison](https://news.wisc.edu/ancient-rocks-reveal-evidence-of-the-first-continents-and-crust-recycling-processes-on-earth)). |
| Archean, 4.0-2.5 billion years ago | Oxygen **under one millionth of today's level**; CO₂ and methane **10-10,000×** modern levels ([Catling & Zahnle 2020](https://faculty.washington.edu/dcatling/Catling2020_and_Zahnle_Archean_Atmosphere_Review.pdf)). An **orange organic haze** (from methane) shrouds the planet at times. NASA's Giada Arney calls Archean Earth "the most alien planet we have geochemical data for" ([astrobiology.com](https://astrobiology.com/2017/02/08/studying-ancient-earth-to-understand-hazy-exoplanets/)). |
| ~3.5 billion years ago | Oldest widely accepted fossils: microbial mats (**stromatolites**). Living stromatolites still grow at **Shark Bay, Australia**, which works as a filmable stand-in. |
| **2.4-2.1 billion years ago** | **The Great Oxidation Event.** Cyanobacteria (photosynthetic microbes) flood the air with oxygen. The rock record shows it as the disappearance of a sulfur-isotope signal. The **ozone layer** begins to form, and with it the **stratosphere** ([Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/othersciences/paleoclimatology/great-oxidation-event-04698.html)). |
| ~800-540 million years ago | Second rise of oxygen (Neoproterozoic Oxygenation Event), close to modern levels. The ozone shield thickens enough for life to move onto land later. |

**Today's layers** (on-screen labels): troposphere 0-~12 km (weather) · stratosphere ~12-50 km (ozone) · mesosphere ~50-85 km · thermosphere ~85-600 km (aurora, ISS orbit ~400 km) · exosphere. The "edge of space" (Kármán line) is 100 km. From the ISS at sunset the bands are visible: **orange troposphere, pink-white stratosphere, blue mesosphere** ([UCAR](https://scied.ucar.edu/image/earths-atmosphere-iss)).

## Phase 6: Earth's transformations before humans

| When (years ago) | Transformation |
|---|---|
| 4.6-4.0 billion | **Hadean**: magma ocean, heavy impacts, first crust, first oceans. |
| 4.0-2.5 billion | **Archean**: first continents, first life, methane haze. |
| 2.4-2.1 billion | **Great Oxidation Event**, possibly followed by a global glaciation (Huronian). |
| ~1.8-1.6 billion | Supercontinent **Nuna (Columbia)**. |
| ~1.8-0.8 billion | The "boring billion": stable climate, slow evolution of complex cells. |
| ~1.1-0.75 billion | Supercontinent **Rodinia**. |
| ~717-660 million | **Sturtian "Snowball Earth"**: ice to the equator for ~57 million years. |
| ~645-635 million | **Marinoan Snowball Earth** ([Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/how-animals-may-have-conquered-snowball-earth-180981340/)). |
| 635-538.8 million | **Ediacaran**: first large, soft-bodied animals. |
| **538.8 million** | **Cambrian explosion**: nearly all animal body plans appear (~540-518 million years ago). |
| ~470 million | Plants colonise land. |
| ~445 million | Mass extinction 1, **end-Ordovician** (glaciation). |
| ~372-359 million | Mass extinction 2, **Late Devonian**. |
| ~335-175 million | Supercontinent **Pangaea**. |
| **~252 million** | Mass extinction 3, **end-Permian**, "the Great Dying" (~90% of marine species). |
| ~201 million | Mass extinction 4, **end-Triassic**, which opened the age of dinosaurs. |
| ~175 million onward | Pangaea breaks up and the Atlantic opens. |
| **66 million** | Mass extinction 5, **end-Cretaceous**: the Chicxulub asteroid impact (Yucatán) ends the non-avian dinosaurs. |
| ~50 million | India collides with Asia, the Himalayas rise. |
| ~2.6 million | Ice-age cycles begin. |
| ~300,000 | *Homo sapiens* appears, where this film ends. |

Mass-extinction dates are given as rounded consensus values. Popular sources vary by a few million years ([BioNumbers "Big Five"](https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=112753), [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/mass-extinctions)).

**Plate motion data:** Cao, Collins, Pisarevsky, Flament, Li, Hasterok & Müller (2024), *Geoscience Frontiers* 101922, published a full-plate reconstruction **1.8 billion years ago to present** covering Nuna, Rodinia and Gondwana/Pangaea. The model is downloadable from Zenodo ([EarthByte](https://www.earthbyte.org/geoscience-frontiers-earths-tectonic-and-plate-boundary-evolution-over-1-8-billion-years/)). This is the best open source for an accurate "continents dancing" sequence; see `code-to-borrow.md` for the tooling.

## Framing: "six days" and science

The six-phase structure echoes the six days of Genesis. The best-known attempt to map them onto cosmology is physicist **Gerald Schroeder**'s (*The Science of God*, 1997). He argues that relativistic time dilation makes six "days" in the reference frame of the early universe equal ~15 billion Earth years, with each day half as long as the one before ([My Jewish Learning](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/reading-modern-science-into-genesis/)). His figures predate the 13.8-billion-year age, and the argument is disputed by scientists such as Victor Stenger ([Infidels.org](https://infidels.org/library/modern/vic-stenger-schrev/)).

Recommendation: let the film present the **science as science** (dates, evidence, uncertainty) and the **six-phase frame as the film's own narrative structure**, without claiming NASA or the data endorse an interpretation. That keeps it credible to both religious and scientific audiences.

## Honesty rules for this film

1. **Label every image by type**: *observation* (a real photo or measurement), *data map*, *simulation*, or *artist's illustration*. Nobody has photographed the Big Bang, a Population III star, or the Hadean Earth. Those shots are always illustrations or simulations.
2. For the earliest eras, use the **real observations that stand in for them**: the CMB, deep fields, Cassiopeia A, HL Tauri, Shark Bay stromatolites, Jack Hills zircons.
3. Present disputed claims as disputed: Population III "evidence", the date of the Moon impact, the earliest life.
4. Never place the NASA "meatball" logo or insignia in the film. It is protected even though NASA imagery is not (see `image-sources.md`).
